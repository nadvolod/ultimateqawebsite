Most business owners I talk to have the same problem with AI. They know it matters, they have tried ChatGPT, and they still can't point to a single dollar it has made them.

That isn't because AI doesn't work. It's because they started with the tool instead of the money.

This post is the process I use with clients to find where AI will pay for itself first. You can do it yourself in an afternoon.

## Start with where money leaks, not with AI

Every business loses money in a few predictable places:

- **Slow replies.** An enquiry comes in, and by the time someone answers, the customer has bought elsewhere.
- **Forgotten follow-ups.** Quotes go out and nobody chases them.
- **Expensive hours on cheap work.** You, or your best people, spend the week on admin a machine could do.
- **Customers who would buy again** but never hear from you.

Before you look at a single AI tool, write down which of these you recognise. Be specific: "We answer website enquiries the next morning" is useful. "We could be more efficient" isn't.

## Put a number on each leak

For each leak, make a rough estimate. It doesn't need to be precise; it needs to be honest.

- How many enquiries, quotes or customers does it touch each month?
- What is one of them worth to you?
- What share do you think you're losing?

## Check that AI is actually good at it

AI is good at work that is:

1. **Repetitive.** The same kind of question, email or document, again and again.
2. **Text-shaped.** Reading, writing, summarising, sorting.
3. **Checkable.** A person can glance at the result and see whether it's right.

AI is bad at work that needs judgement it hasn't been given, relationships, or decisions where a mistake is expensive and hard to spot.

So answering common customer questions is a great fit. Deciding whether to give a big client a discount is not.

If your biggest leak passes these three checks, you have your first project.

## Run a two-week test, not a transformation

Don't buy a platform. Don't sign a year-long contract. Run a small test:

- **Week one:** set up the simplest version that could work. Often that's a well-written prompt and a shared document, not software.
- **Week two:** use it on real work, and keep a simple tally: how much time it saved, how many leads or quotes it touched, and what went wrong.

At the end, you'll know whether it's worth doing properly. Most of the value in AI comes from this kind of small, measured step, repeated.

## What to do this week

1. List your money leaks.
2. Put a rough number on each.
3. Pick the biggest one that passes the three checks.
4. Book two weeks to test it.

That's it. No strategy deck, no new software. Just one leak, fixed and measured.
