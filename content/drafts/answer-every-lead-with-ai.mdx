A customer fills in your contact form at 9pm. They've also filled in your competitor's. Whoever answers first, with a useful answer, usually gets the call.

If your reply goes out the next morning, you're often already second.

An AI assistant can close that gap. Not by pretending to be you, but by answering the common questions straight away and handing anyone ready to buy to a real person.

## What the assistant should do, and what it shouldn't

Decide this before you touch any tool.

**Let the assistant:**

- Answer the questions you get every week: prices, availability, areas you cover, how the process works.
- Ask the questions you'd ask anyway: what they need, when, and their budget.
- Book a call or pass the conversation to you.

**Keep for a human:**

- Custom quotes and discounts.
- Complaints.
- Anyone who says they're ready to buy.

The assistant's job is to make sure nobody waits, not to close the deal.

## Step 1: write down your ten most common questions

This is the most important hour of the whole project. Go through your inbox, your phone notes and your team's heads, and write down:

- the ten questions customers ask most, and
- your best answer to each, in your own words.

That document is the whole brain of your assistant. If the answers are vague, the assistant will be vague.

## Step 2: decide where it answers

Start with the channel where enquiries already arrive and wait longest. For most businesses that's one of:

- the contact form on your website,
- a chat box on your website, or
- email.

Pick one. You can add others later.

## Step 3: set the rules for handing over

Write simple rules for when the assistant stops and passes the conversation to you, for example:

- the customer asks for a quote,
- the customer is unhappy,
- the question isn't in your list, or
- the customer asks for a person.

When it hands over, it should send you the whole conversation so the customer never has to repeat themselves.

## Step 4: test it on yourself

Before it talks to a single customer, send it twenty enquiries you've received in the past. Check:

- Is every answer correct?
- Does it sound like your business?
- Does it hand over when it should?

Fix your question list wherever it gets things wrong. This step catches most problems.

## Step 5: go live and read every conversation for two weeks

For the first two weeks, read every conversation. You'll find questions you forgot and answers that need work. After that, a weekly check is enough.

## Which tool to use

There are many tools that do this, and the right one depends on where your enquiries arrive and what you already use. I'll keep a plain-English comparison in [Choose the right tools](/blog/), but the tool matters far less than the question list from step 1.

## What to do this week

Write your ten questions and answers. Even if you never set up an assistant, you'll have a better FAQ page and faster replies from your own team.
