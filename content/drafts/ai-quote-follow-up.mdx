You spend an hour on a quote, send it, and hear nothing. A week later you mean to chase it, but something urgent comes up. A month later the customer has hired someone else.

Most businesses don't lose these sales because of price. They lose them because nobody followed up.

Following up is exactly the kind of work AI is good at: frequent, text-shaped and easy to check. Here's how to set it up so every quote gets chased, without sounding like a robot.

## Decide your follow-up rhythm

Before any AI, decide what good follow-up looks like for your business. For example:

- **Day 2:** a short note checking the quote arrived and offering to answer questions.
- **Day 7:** something useful: an answer to a common worry, a relevant example of your work, or a reminder of what's included.
- **Day 14:** a simple, polite question about whether they're still interested.

Adjust the days to how quickly your customers usually decide.

## Keep a simple list of open quotes

AI can only follow up on quotes it knows about. You need one place that lists every open quote with:

- the customer's name and email,
- what you quoted and the price,
- the date you sent it, and
- anything personal from the conversation, such as a deadline or a concern they raised.

A spreadsheet is enough. Most quoting and CRM tools already store this.

## Let AI draft, and you send

Each morning, give the AI the quotes that are due a follow-up, along with:

- your follow-up rhythm,
- a few follow-up emails you've written yourself that worked well, and
- the personal notes for each customer.

Ask it to draft one email per quote in your voice. You read each draft, tweak it if needed, and send it. It takes minutes, not an afternoon.

## Why you should still press send yourself, at first

It's tempting to let AI send the emails automatically. Don't, at least for the first month. You'll catch drafts that are too pushy, miss context, or get a detail wrong. Once the drafts are reliably good, you can automate the simple day-2 note and keep reviewing the rest.

## Track what happens

Add one column to your list: **result**. Won, lost, or still open. After a couple of months you'll see which follow-ups get replies and which don't, and you can feed that back into your examples.

## What to do this week

Make a list of every quote you've sent in the last month that hasn't been decided. Ask AI to draft a short, friendly follow-up for each, using your notes. Send the ones you're happy with. You may win back work you'd already written off.
