Every software company now says its product has "AI agents". It's become one of those words that means everything and nothing. If you're deciding what to buy, that's a problem.

Here's what an agent actually is, without the jargon.

## A chatbot answers. An agent acts.

When you use ChatGPT, you ask a question and it gives you an answer. You then decide what to do with it. That's a **chatbot**, or an assistant.

An **agent** is given a goal instead of a question, and it takes steps on its own to reach it. It can use tools: look things up, send an email, update a spreadsheet, book a meeting. It checks what happened and decides the next step.

A simple way to remember it:

- **Chatbot:** "Write a reply to this customer."
- **Agent:** "Handle new enquiries: answer the common ones, book calls for serious buyers, and tell me about anything unusual."

## What an agent is made of

Under the hood, almost every agent has the same four parts:

1. **A model.** The AI that reads, writes and reasons, like the one behind ChatGPT or Claude.
2. **Instructions.** What the agent is for, what it may and may not do, and when to stop and ask a person.
3. **Tools.** The things it's allowed to touch: your inbox, calendar, website, spreadsheets.
4. **A loop.** It takes a step, looks at the result, and decides what to do next until the job is done.

When a vendor says "agent", ask which tools it can use and what it does when it isn't sure. Those two answers tell you most of what you need to know.

## When you need an agent, and when you don't

Most businesses don't need an agent first. They need a good prompt, a clear process, and a person in the loop.

An agent starts to make sense when:

- the work has several steps across different tools,
- it happens often enough that doing it by hand is a real cost, and
- you can clearly describe what "done" looks like.

If you can't write the process down on one page, an agent won't fix it. It will just do the confusion faster.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- What exactly can it access, and can I limit that?
- What does it do when it isn't sure? Does it ask a person?
- Can I see a log of everything it did?
- How do I switch it off?
- What does it cost per month at my volume, not the demo's?

If the seller can't answer these clearly, keep looking.
