You're busy, the work keeps piling up, and the obvious answer is to hire. Sometimes that's the right call. Sometimes AI could take most of that work for a fraction of the cost. And often the best answer is somewhere in between.

Here's the test I walk business owners through before they post a job ad.

## Write down the job as tasks, not a title

"An assistant" or "a marketing person" is too vague to judge. List what this person would actually do each week, for example:

- reply to customer emails,
- write two social posts,
- update the spreadsheet of orders,
- chase unpaid invoices,
- call new leads.

Aim for ten to fifteen tasks. You'll test each one separately.

## The five questions

Ask these about each task.

1. **Does it happen often, in roughly the same way?** Repetition is where AI shines.
2. **Is it mostly reading or writing?** Emails, documents, summaries and posts are a good fit. Physical work and phone relationships are not.
3. **Can someone check the result in under a minute?** If yes, mistakes are cheap to catch.
4. **Is a mistake cheap?** A slightly clumsy social post is fine. A wrong invoice to your biggest customer is not.
5. **Do you have examples of it done well?** AI copies good examples far better than it follows vague instructions.

## Score each task

- **Four or five yes answers:** AI can probably do most of it, with a person checking.
- **Two or three:** AI can help, but a person still does the work.
- **Zero or one:** keep it human.

## Read the result

Add up where your tasks landed.

- **Mostly AI:** you may not need a hire yet. Try handing those tasks to AI for a month and see how much time comes back.
- **A mix:** hire, but hire for the human tasks, and give the new person AI tools for the rest. You'll likely need fewer hours, or get far more from the same hours.
- **Mostly human:** hire. AI won't fix this one, and pretending it will just delays you.

## Common mistakes

- **Handing over judgement calls.** AI drafting the email is fine; AI deciding who gets a refund isn't.
- **Skipping the check.** Every AI task needs a person who glances at the output, at least at first.
- **Measuring nothing.** Note how long the tasks take now, so you can see whether AI actually saved time.

## What to do this week

List the tasks for the role you're thinking of hiring, run the five questions, and count the scores. It takes about thirty minutes and could save you a salary, or confirm the hire with a lot more confidence.
